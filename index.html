Types of Hemorrhage

What is hemorrhage?

Hemorrhage (bleeding) is the escape of blood from the heart or blood vessels, either out of the body or into it.

An adult has about 5 litres of blood. Losing a large amount quickly leads to shock, which is an emergency.

Types of hemorrhage is classified into 4 parts

  1. Based on source of bleeding
  2. Based on types of hemorrhage
  3. Based on duration of hemorrhage
  4. Based on onset of bleeding

Further classification

1. Based on source of bleeding

Which blood vessel is bleeding. There are 3 types.

Arterial

Bright red blood that spurts in jets with each heartbeat. High pressure, so blood is lost fast.

Venous

Dark red blood that flows out steadily. Lower pressure.

Capillary

Slow oozing from the whole surface, as in a graze. Usually stops on its own.

2. Based on types of hemorrhage

Can the blood be seen? There are 3 types.

Revealed

Blood is visible outside the body, for example a wound, nosebleed or vomiting blood.

Concealed

Blood collects inside the body and cannot be seen, for example a ruptured spleen. Suspected from signs of shock.

Initially concealed but later revealed

Starts inside, then shows outside, for example a bleeding ulcer that later causes black stools.

3. Based on duration of hemorrhage

How fast is blood lost? There are 3 types.

Acute

Sudden, heavy loss in a short time. Can cause shock.

Chronic

Slow, repeated small losses over weeks or months. Leads to anaemia, for example from piles.

Acute on chronic

A sudden bleed on top of slow, ongoing loss. The patient is already anaemic.

4. Based on onset of bleeding

When does the bleeding start? There are 3 types.

Primary

At the time of the injury or operation.

Reactionary

Within 24 hours, usually in the first few hours. Blood pressure rises again, or a tie (ligature) slips.

Secondary

About 7 to 14 days later, usually because of infection.
